\begin{abstract}
We conduct the first large-scale evaluation of machine-generated imputations for a damaged Ancient Greek papyrus. A large number of conjectures are generated by a non-ergodic ``Metropolis-in-Gibbs'' random walk through the space of formally possible restorations. \section{Introduction}
\label{introduction}

Conjectural text criticism is one of the most methodologically controversial parts of classical philology, but also one of the most well-established.\footnote{See \citet{olson2020tereus} for a recent critique of what the author sees as the shaky methodological foundation of conjectural text criticism.} Since Hellenistic times, scholars have made informed conjectures as to what the lacunae of damaged or corrupted texts could have said, conjectures that are sometimes vindicated or falsified by newly found papyri and palimpsests.\footnote{For example, Stiebitz had conjectured ἰ]ο̣κ[ό]λ̣π[ων (``purple-girdled''; square brackets indicate papyrus edges) in a poem by Sappho, a reading later proved correct by a papyrus. \citep{hammerstaedt2009cologne} Inversely, given ]κ’ ἐπιβο̣[  Furley 2013 conjectured γυναῖ]κ’ ἐπιβο̣[υλον, ``treacherous woman'', which a new discovery reading φάρμακ’ ἐπιβο̣[ (``treacherous potions'') falsified. \citep{goldberg2023menander}} Large language models like \citet{cullhed2026stoicheiacharacterlevelmaskeddiffusion} hold the promise to let scholars avoid criticisms of subjectivity and anachronism by shifting the basic method from the ingenuity of individual editors \citep{conte2013ope} to statistical treatments of large arrays of machine generated conjectures. With this radical change of method, the research question ``what is the best imputation?'' obsolesces in favour of ``what are the tendencies of this set of $N$ machine imputations?''. Like \textit{New philology} fragmented the medieval text into a ``manuscript matrix'' that can only be studied by computational methods \citep{nichols1990philology}, we fragment the ingenious conjecture into an ``imputation matrix''.

As a case study, we use the papyrus \textit{P.Oxy 5292} first published in \citet{slattery2016tereus}, which preserves a fragmentary continuation of a preserved quote from Sophocles' otherwise lost play \textit{Tereus}, a staging of the myth of Philomela (see \autoref{papyrus}). The tendencies we will look for in this article are topics.

\section{Research questions}
\label{questions}

First some definitions. Let us in this context define a `topic' as a probability distribution over a set vocabulary of lemmata. Given a baseline corpus, the corpus of imputations' `lift' \textit{r} is defined as the ratio between its mean lemma rates and that of the baseline's. We say that a certain topic $t$ `stands out' in the imputation corpus relative to a baseline when the credible interval of the lift is either entirely greater than or entirely less than 1. 

The most pertinent baseline is Sophocles' other works, but gradually zooming out we will also consider the other two preserved authors of the genre of tragedy, as well as the Attic dialect as a whole, including prose. We are now ready to ask: are there topics that stand out across chains and iterations of generated papyrus imputations? 

\section{Data}
\label{data}

The imputations come from a complex walk over restorations
of the seventeen damaged verses (vv.~13--29), whose details are left to \autoref{walk}.
Twenty-four chains start from restorations as far apart as possible, any
two differing in 89--100\,\% of their restored words; each runs 10\,000
steps, and a state is recorded every ten steps after a burn-in of 300,
giving 23\,280 restorations.
Every text is lemmatised and reduced to a bag of lemmata over a vocabulary of
3\,128 lemmata.

A \textbf{document} is one whole restoration of the seventeen verses, on
average 27 topic-bearing lemmata; only restored text counts, never the
surviving letters. The baseline is Sophocles himself: his extant trimeters,
cut into 444 windows of seventeen consecutive verses, the shape of the
lacuna. Two further baselines tell what is unsophoclean from what is merely
untragic: Aeschylus and Euripides (1\,174 windows) and Classical Attic, the
Attic authors before Alexander (23\,328 windows), both in windows of 30
lemmata.

\section{Models and methods}
\label{models}

\subsection{Hard topics: conjugate posterior}

Every lemma of the vocabulary belongs to exactly one of 34 semantic clusters
(Word2Vec, \citealt{mikolov2013efficient}, on the lemmatised Greek corpus,
Ward's method cut at $K = 40$, two rare-lemma and four function-word clusters
dropped), named by the English glosses of the three non-cognate lemmata
nearest its centroid, for example \textit{lament-pitiable-groan}
(\cref{tab:topics}). A document's topic counts $\mathbf n$ are then
\[\mathbf n \mid \theta \sim \mathrm{Multinomial}(N, \theta), \qquad
  \theta \sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}(\kappa \mathbf m),\]
where $N$ is the document's own length, $\mathbf m$ is Sophocles' topic
profile and $\kappa = 1$: a restoration is a priori Sophoclean, but the prior
is worth a single lemma, so a document of twenty lemmata is 95\,\% data. A
symmetric prior would pull short documents towards the uniform profile and
so fabricate lift for topics they simply lack. The posterior
$\theta \mid \mathbf n \sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}(\kappa \mathbf m + \mathbf n)$
is exact and is sampled by normalised gamma draws.

\subsection{Soft topics: MCMC posterior}

Hard clusters put every occurrence of a lemma into one topic, although a
frequent word can serve several. The soft model gives each topic a
distribution $\phi_k$ over the vocabulary (\cref{fig:phi}), fitted once by a seeded
latent Dirichlet allocation \citep{blei2003latent} on Aeschylus and Euripides
only (tomotopy, \citealt{lee2022tomotopy}),\footnote{In LDA each
document $d$ has topic shares $\theta_d \sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha)$ and
each topic a word distribution $\phi_k \sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}(\beta_k)$;
every lemma token first draws a topic $z \sim \theta_d$, then the lemma from
$\phi_z$. Tomotopy fits this by collapsed Gibbs sampling
\citep{griffiths2004finding}: $\theta$ and $\phi$ are integrated out, and each
token's topic, a discrete variable that HMC cannot sample since it needs a
gradient, is redrawn in turn from its full conditional given all the others, and $\alpha$, one concentration per topic, is re-estimated every ten
sweeps by maximising the marginal likelihood. After 10\,000 sweeps of
burn-in, twenty states 500 sweeps apart are kept, and in each
$\phi_{k,w} \propto n_{k,w} + \beta_{k,w}$ is read off the counts $n$. Seeding changes only the prior: a lemma of
cluster $k$ has $\beta_{k,w} = 200$, two hundred pseudo-occurrences in its own
topic, and $0.01$ in every other, while unclustered lemmata and the four free
topics get $0.01$ throughout. Here $K = 38$, and the documents
are the 1\,174 windows of Aeschylus and Euripides.} so that Sophocles is a test corpus
like the restorations. Each hard cluster seeds one topic with a strong prior
on its lemmata, and four free topics take up what the clusters miss.
\textgreek{θεός}, god, which the hard model puts in \textit{god-true-lord}
alone, is now split between two topics: given the word, the free topic
\textit{god-evil-word} has probability 0.82 and \textit{god-true-lord} 0.16
($\phi_{k,w}$ normalised over $k$). The LDA also learns the prior on a
document's topic shares, a Dirichlet($\alpha$) with one concentration per
topic, the asymmetric prior that \citet{wallach2009rethinking} find to fit
topic models better than a symmetric one: below 1 for every seeded topic (0.04--0.57) and between 4 and 9 for
the free ones, which a typical document is thus expected to be mostly made of.

The fit has two stages, and the second never feeds back into the first (a
cut model, \citealt{plummer2015cuts}; \cref{fig:cut}). Given one state $(\phi, \alpha)$ of
the LDA's chain, a document's lemmata are drawn from the mixture
$\sum_k \theta_k \phi_k$ with $\theta \sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha)$, and
the posterior
\[p(\theta \mid w_{1:N}) \propto \mathrm{Dirichlet}(\theta \mid \alpha)
  \prod_{n=1}^{N} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \theta_k\, \phi_{k w_n}\]
has no closed form. It is sampled with NUTS in Stan
\citep{carpenter2017stan}, one document at a time, by the program in \cref{fig:stan}. This is done for each of the twenty states, and the twenty posteriors
are pooled, so that the intervals carry the uncertainty in $\phi$ as well as
in $\theta$ (diagnostics in \autoref{diagnostics}).

\subsection{Comparing the imputations with Sophocles}

A set of documents gives two posteriors. The \textbf{mean profile}
$\bar\theta$ averages the documents' posteriors draw by draw and says what
the imputations put into the lacunae on average. The \textbf{random document}
is the equal mixture of the documents' posteriors; set against a random
Sophoclean window, $P(\theta_k^{\text{doc}} > \theta_k^{\text{window}})$
measures how far the two populations overlap (0.5 if not at all apart). The
\textbf{lift} of topic $k$ is the ratio of the imputations' mean share to
Sophocles'. The
restorations are not independent, since successive states of a chain share
most of their words; the 90\,\% intervals for a lift therefore resample
whole chains (and Sophocles by window), and for the soft model each replicate
also draws one of the twenty states of $\phi$ and adds back the posterior
noise of the two profiles. A topic \textbf{stands out} when this interval
lies entirely above 1 or entirely below it.

\paragraph{Hard topics.} The imputations write tragedy: of nine genres their
topic profile is nearest to tragedy.\footnote{Jensen--Shannon divergence 0.052 bits,
against 0.089 for comedy and 0.140 for epic.} It is not, however,
Sophocles' profile (\cref{fig:lift}). Six topics stand out above it, led
by \textit{marriage-couch-bed} (lift 2.13), and fifteen below, the sea and its
rivers almost vanishing (\textit{ship-sail-hull} 0.12,
\textit{river-plain-afar} 0.17).

The further baselines separate the unsophoclean from the untragic
(\cref{tab:key}).
\textit{god-true-lord}, \textit{wrong-vengeance-err} and
\textit{child-before-bestow} stand out above all three (lifts 1.26--6.48).
\textit{marriage-couch-bed} stands out against Sophocles (2.13) and Classical
Attic (3.42) but not against Aeschylus and Euripides (0.91, interval
0.71--1.12), who use that vocabulary more than Sophocles does. Lemma by
lemma, almost half the excess of \textit{god-true-lord} is a single word:
\textgreek{θεός} occurs 45.7 times per thousand topic lemmata in the
restorations against 15.6 in Sophocles. That of \textit{wrong-vengeance-err}
is spread over the matter of the play: \textgreek{χάρις}, \textgreek{δίκη},
\textgreek{πάθος}, \textgreek{πόνος} and \textgreek{τιμωρός}, favour,
justice, suffering, toil and the avenger.

\paragraph{Soft topics.} Under the soft model three topics stand out above
Sophocles and sixteen below. \textit{persuade-man-king} leads by far (lift
2.62, interval 1.92--3.36), before \textit{god-true-lord} (1.36) and
\textit{child-before-bestow} (1.29). It stands out above all three
baselines, Aeschylus and Euripides (2.69) and Classical Attic (1.54) too,
as does \textit{child-before-bestow}: an excess over Greek at large, not
only over Sophocles. The excess of \textit{persuade-man-king} is one of
oaths: per thousand lemmata the
restorations have \textgreek{ὄμνυμι}, swear, 16.6 times against 0.2 in
Sophocles and \textgreek{ὅρκος}, oath, 10.2 against 0.4, a theme the papyrus
itself sets with \textgreek{ὅρκον γάρ} at the start of v.~23. Nor is it
one chain's: 22 of the 24 chains lie above Sophocles over their whole run,
though they wander (0.85--7.45, \cref{fig:trajectory}). Of the other topics
that stand out under the hard model, \textit{marriage-couch-bed} falls back
to 1.26 and \textit{wrong-vengeance-err} to 1.09, both with intervals that
include 1, and \textit{god-true-lord} shrinks from 1.83 to 1.36. The reason
is visible in $\phi$: the most frequent words of tragedy leave their
clusters for the free topics, which take three quarters of the imputations'
mean profile, and \textgreek{θεός}, which carried much of the hard model's
excess of the god topic, goes mostly to \textit{god-evil-word}.

\paragraph{Robustness.} The two rounds of starts, chosen separately (8
and 16 chains), give hard log lifts that correlate at 0.73. The judge
matters more: from Eric Cullhed's reconstructions, a pilot with Claude
Sonnet 5.5 as judge correlates with the Qwen profile at only 0.30, yet
\textit{persuade-man-king} and \textit{wrong-vengeance-err} stay above
Sophocles under every judge. The soft lifts barely move between the states
of $\phi$ but scale with the prior on $\theta$, whose pseudo-lemmata compete
with a document's thirty: a flat Dirichlet(1) shrinks every log lift to
about half its learned-prior value. The order barely moves (log lifts
correlate at 0.89--0.95), and \textit{persuade-man-king} leads under all
four priors tried.

\section{The Metropolis walk}\label{walk}

The restorations are the states of a Metropolis-within-Gibbs walk over the
seventeen damaged verses; \cref{fig:walk} shows one step. The
character-level diffusion model Stoicheia
\citep{cullhed2026stoicheiacharacterlevelmaskeddiffusion} proposes new text for
a span of one verse, hard formal checks discard impossible candidates, and a
surviving candidate is accepted with probability $\min(1, r)$. The ratio $r$
has two parts:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item small calibration terms that bring the restorer's habits
(finite verbs, particles, word repetition) to Sophocles' rates,
    \item and an LLM judge's preference for the more coherent of the old and the new line.
\end{enumerate}

Importantly, nothing in the walk rewards Sophoclean style as such, so it remains a source of imputations rather than a search for the most Sophoclean text.

Twenty-four chains start from restorations as far apart in words as
possible, chosen in two rounds of eight and sixteen: each start is the
candidate whose smallest word distance to the starts already chosen is
largest. One is Eric Cullhed's reconstruction, the others late states of
earlier chains. Every chain runs 10\,000 steps; after a burn-in of 300 steps
every tenth state is kept. The
chains do not mix within that budget (\autoref{diagnostics}).

\section{Sampler and chain diagnostics}\label{diagnostics}

\paragraph{The soft model in Stan.} Every document was fitted separately
for each of the twenty states of $(\phi, \alpha)$, with four chains of 500
warm-up and 1\,000 sampling iterations (4\,000 draws) at a target acceptance
of 0.99: with the learned $\alpha_k$ below 1 the prior density is unbounded at
the faces of the simplex, and Stan's default of 0.8 left divergences in
nearly every document. The table counts the documents flagged within one
state (the median over the twenty states), and the worst values over all
states. The soft model was fitted while the last chain stood at step
7\,500, hence 250 imputations fewer than the hard model reads.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrrr}
\toprule
& & \multicolumn{4}{c}{Flagged documents per state (median)} & Largest & Smallest \\
\cmidrule(lr){3-6}
Set & Documents & $\hat R > 1.01$ & ESS $< 400$ & Divergent & Tree depth & $\hat R$ & bulk ESS \\
\midrule
Imputations & 23\,030 & 1\,921 & 568 & 10 & 543 & 1.19 & 15 \\
Sophocles & 443 & 60 & 29 & 0 & 20 & 1.22 & 13 \\
Aeschylus, Euripides & 1\,174 & 165 & 93 & 1 & 36 & 1.32 & 10 \\
Classical Attic & 23\,328 & 2\,540 & 1\,088 & 12 & 600 & 1.50 & 7 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent About a tenth of the documents have, at a given state, a topic
whose chains disagree: a seeded topic with $\alpha_k$ of 0.1 or 0.2 that the
document does not use piles its mass against $\theta_k = 0$, with a thin
tail that a chain may stray into. Under a flat Dirichlet(1) prior the same
documents sample with hardly a flag. The lifts average over hundreds or
thousands of documents, in which one document's Monte Carlo error is
averaged away, and the twenty states, each a separate run, agree
(\autoref{results}). One imputation and one Sophoclean window, refitted with
rstan, reproduce the posterior means of their five largest topics to within
0.01.

\paragraph{The walk.} The chains are compared by the Gelman--Rubin $\hat R$
of every topic's share \citep[§11.4]{gelman2013bda3}, in each round of
starts, over all twenty-four chains, and in the earlier set from Eric
Cullhed's starts.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrlr}
\toprule
& & Topics with & & \textit{wrong-vengeance-err}, \\
Starts & Chains & $\hat R > 1.1$ & Largest $\hat R$ & chain lifts \\
\midrule
First round & 8 & 22 of 34 & 3.07 (\textit{fire-flame-flash}) & 0.98--2.04 \\
Second round & 16 & 27 of 34 & 1.77 (\textit{rear-sprout-bloom}) & 0.45--1.88 \\
Both rounds & 24 & 27 of 34 & 2.99 (\textit{fire-flame-flash}) & 0.51--2.01 \\
\midrule
Eric Cullhed's & 16 & 28 of 34 & 1.51 (\textit{lament-pitiable-groan}) & 0.74--3.00 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The chains have not mixed in 10\,000 steps, whether or not they
share an origin, which is why both models' intervals resample whole
chains (chain lifts: 5th to 95th percentile). There is little systematic drift
after the burn-in: over windows of 1\,000 steps the median absolute slope of
a topic's lift is 0.019 per 1\,000 steps, the steepest 0.148
(\textit{marriage-couch-bed}).
