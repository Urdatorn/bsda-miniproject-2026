\begin{abstract}
We conduct the first large-scale evaluation of machine-generated imputations for a damaged Ancient Greek papyrus. A large number of conjectures are generated by a non-ergodic ``Metropolis-in-Gibbs'' random walk through the space of formally possible restorations. Even though the complete space is formally too large to explore due to combinatorial explosion, the joint restrictions of verse and semantic context make it plausible that topic modelling a large sample is still valuable information regarding the lost true topic. As a baseline, we estimate the analytic posterior Dirichlet distribution of unmixed topics. We then fit an amortized latent Dirichlet allocation model and sample from the posterior assuming \textit{mixed} topics, which is the more realistic assumption. 
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{introduction}

Conjectural text criticism is one of the most methodologically controversial parts of classical philology, but also one of the most well-established.\footnote{See \citet{olson2020tereus} for a recent critique of what the author sees as the shaky methodological foundation of conjectural text criticism.} Since Hellenistic times, scholars have made informed conjectures as to what the lacunae of damaged or corrupted texts could have said, conjectures that are sometimes vindicated or falsified by newly found papyri. Large masked-language models (MLM) like \citet{cullhed2026stoicheiacharacterlevelmaskeddiffusion} hold the promise to let scholars avoid criticisms of subjectivity and anachronism by shifting the basic method from the ingenuity of individual editors (what \citet{conte2013ope} calls \textit{ope ingenii}) to statistical treatments of large arrays of machine generated conjectures. With this radical change of method, the research question changes from ``is this particular reconstruction probable'' to ``how probable is it that the true reconstruction is somewhere among these $N$ machine reconstructions'', given very large-dimensional stylometric feature vectors for them and a set of strongly informative priors regarding the distribution of characteristics of the extant corpus. 

As a case study, we use the famous papyrus \textit{P.Oxy 5292} first published in \citet{slattery2016tereus} (see \autoref{papyrus}), which preserves a fragmentary continuation of a preserved quote from Sophocles' otherwise lost play \textit{Tereus}, a staging of the myth of Philomela.

\subsection{Hard topics: conjugate posterior}

Every lemma of the vocabulary belongs to exactly one of $K$ semantic clusters
(Word2Vec on the lemmatised Greek corpus, Ward's method, cut at $K = 40$; two
rare-lemma clusters and four function-word clusters dropped, leaving 34). Each
cluster is named by the English glosses of
the three non-cognate lemmata nearest its centroid, for example \textit{lament-pitiable-groan} or
\textit{marriage-couch-bed} (see \cref{tab:topics}). A \textbf{document} is $N$ consecutive topic-bearing lemmata of a
stream, and its topic counts are

\[\mathbf n \mid \theta \sim \mathrm{Multinomial}(N, \theta), \qquad
  \theta \sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha \mathbf 1).\]

The Dirichlet is conjugate to the multinomial, so the posterior is exact,

\[\theta \mid \mathbf n \sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha \mathbf 1 + \mathbf n).\]

To sample from this posterior in R it is enough to draw from \texttt{rgamma} and normalise.

\section{Metropolis-in-Gibbs non-ergodic random walk}\label{walk}

At each step, the character-level diffusion model Stoicheia \citep{cullhed2026stoicheiacharacterlevelmaskeddiffusion} is making a proposal to a small word span, which is accepted with probability $\operatorname{min}(1, r)$, where the acceptance ratio $r$ depends on both deterministic orthographic, linguistic and stylistic checks based on the known author's use, and the qualitative judgement of an open-source chatbot LLM.

\newpage
\section{The walk and the pipeline, step by step}\label{flowchart}

\autoref{fig:flowchart} lays out one step of the walk of \autoref{walk} as it
was run for every set of imputations in this report, and what happens to the
recorded states afterwards. The judge is Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507 at
$1/T_{\text{judge}} = 4$; the style term is switched off
($1/T_{\text{formal}} = 0$), so the walk samples Stoicheia's own distribution
under the formal constraints and the judge's coherence preference, and nothing
in it selects for Sophoclean style. Sixteen chains start from Eric Cullhed's
eight reconstructions, which are variants of one text; twenty-four more start
from late states of earlier chains chosen so that every two starts differ in
nearly all their restored words, which is what lets the chain diagnostics tell
the walk's own mixing from the starts' kinship. Every chain runs 10\,000 steps
in four segments of 2\,500, each continuing the last state of the one before,
and keeps one state in ten after a burn-in of 300. The recorded states are
lemmatised, reduced to bags of topic-bearing lemmata and compared with
Sophocles by the hard and soft models of \autoref{models}; the chain
checks (drift, $\hat R$, Eric's starts against unrelated starts, and the
Qwen judges against a Claude Sonnet 5.5 pilot) close the pipeline.
